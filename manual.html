The Story So Far

Meet Jeff Lovett: age 44, owner of one powder-blue leisure suit, one gold chain, and an optimism that no amount of evidence has ever dented.

It's 2:30 on a Friday afternoon at Synergex Solutions, where Jeff has spent many years doing something with reports. Tomorrow night at 8:00 is the Westside High reunion, and Jeff intends to show the Class of '61 exactly how well he turned out.

There are, however, a few small problems:

  • Jeff's Pontiac Fiero won't start. It makes a sound like a cricket with a head cold.
  • His suit has a chili stain on it shaped exactly like the state of Ohio.
  • He has $12.75 to his name, and his boss hasn't handed over this week's paycheck.

Your mission, should you choose to accept it (and you paid good money, so you should): get paid, fix the car, clean the suit, and get a good night's sleep. How hard can it be?

Getting Started

System Requirements

Computer: IBM PC, XT, AT, PS/2, Tandy 1000, or 100% compatible.

Memory: 256K. 512K recommended to hold Jeff's ego.

Display: EGA card and color monitor. Sixteen glorious colors, every one of them tasteful.

Operating system: MS-DOS or PC-DOS 2.1 or higher.

Drives: Two 5¼" floppy drives or a hard disk.

Sound: PC speaker supported. AdLib Music Synthesizer Card recommended for the full lounge experience.

Joystick: Not supported. Jeff steers with his heart.

Loading From Floppy Disk

  1. Boot your computer with DOS. Find the A> prompt. Admire it.
  2. Insert Disk 1 in drive A, label side up, and close the latch.
  3. Type JEFF and press ENTER.
  4. When the title screen appears, press ENTER to begin, or type RESTORE to pick up where you left off.

Hard Disk Installation

Insert Disk 1, type A: INSTALL C: and press ENTER. Then type CD \JEFF and JEFF. Installation takes about four minutes, or one cup of coffee. Do not use the office coffee. (See page 11.)

Talking To Jeff

Leisure Suit Jeff understands plain English. Well, plain-ish. Type what you want Jeff to do at the > prompt and press ENTER. You can use capital or small letters. Little words like the and a are fine. Jeff ignores them, just like he ignores his doctor.

LOOKDescribes your surroundings. Do this a lot.
LOOK AT thing  or  X thingExamines something up close. READ works on anything with writing on it.
TAKE thingPicks it up. GET, GRAB and PICK UP work too.
TALK TO personStarts a conversation. Results may vary.
GIVE thing TO personHands something over. Generosity is its own reward. Sometimes it's also points.
USE thing ON thingCombines two things. PUT thing IN thing works the same way.
OPEN, SEARCHFor doors, drawers, cushions and other places things hide.
WEAR, EAT, DRINK, BUY, CLEAN, KICK, SMELL, SIT, SLEEP, FLIRTJeff knows lots of verbs. If you'd do it in real life, try typing it.
INVENTORY or ILists what you're carrying, wearing and how much cash you have.
HELP   HINTHELP lists commands. HINT gives a gentle nudge when you're really stuck.

Shortcuts

Press the ↑ and ↓ arrow keys to recall earlier commands. You can also click any name listed under the picture and it will be typed in for you.

A Sample Session

Here's an actual conversation between a player and Jeff, reproduced with Jeff's permission.

Your Screen At A Glance

  • 1The status line: your score out of 130, plus the day, the time and your cash.
  • 2The picture: where Jeff is, painted in sixteen genuine EGA colors. It changes as you change things.
  • 3The panel: the name of the place, everything worth a closer look under You see, the Exits you can walk to, and places across town you can Travel to.
  • 4The story: what Jeff does, what people say, and how many points you just earned.
  • 5The prompt: type here. The ♪ button turns the music and sound on or off.
Pro Tip

Everything listed under You see can be looked at, and most of it can be used for something. Our playtesters found that the players who LOOKed at everything finished with the most points and the fewest deaths. The playtesters who didn't are no longer with us. (They moved to Phoenix.)

Getting Around Town

Inside a building, type GO and the name of an exit, such as GO LOBBY. But a man with places to be doesn't need a hallway. From anywhere, type GO TO and a destination: GO TO HOME, GO TO WORK, or GO TO COSTCO. Type MAP to list every place Jeff knows.

The #42 Bus

TAKE BUS TO place costs $2.00 a ride, exact change. You'll meet interesting people. Many of them have soup.

The Fiero

Once the Fiero runs again, DRIVE TO place is free, fast, and enormously attractive to women (Jeff's opinion).

The Clock

Every move takes a little time, and travel takes more. The clock on your status line is real. Stores keep real hours. Type TIME to check.

Places Jeff Knows

Synergex Solutions (work): lobby, cubicles, break room, and Mr. Brumley's office.
Shady Pines Apartments (home): the parking lot, Jeff's bachelor pad and his bathroom.
Costco: the parking lot, entrance, aisles, tire center and food court.

Jeff's Rules For Living

There are 130 points in Act I. You can finish with fewer, but your mother will be disappointed. Jeff has kindly written down his personal philosophy, which will lead you to every one of them without spoiling the fun. (For the complete, spoiler-filled solution, see the official strategy guide sold separately.)

Synergex Solutions43 points
Shady Pines Apartments17 points
Costco40 points
The Victory Lap30 points
Total130 points
At The Office 43 pts
  1. Read your mail. Management announces important policy changes in memos nobody reads. Be the one person who does.
  2. Paperwork doesn't walk. A report that stays on your desk can't be turned in anywhere else. Carry it with you.
  3. Computers print. Printers complain. When a machine gives you an error message, work out what it's hungry for and feed it. Office supplies have a way of ending up holding the furniture together.
  4. Percussive maintenance is a time-honored office tradition. It never fixes anything. It is still worth doing.
  5. Somebody's birthday is everybody's opportunity. Leftover cake belongs to whoever gets there first. (Sorry, Deb.)
  6. Show some flair. Corporate culture demands it. Picking it up is not the same as putting it on.
  7. Everybody has a price. A man won't part with his favorite possession for nothing, but a man who hasn't had a corner piece in eleven years can be reasoned with.
  8. A report isn't finished until it's in one piece. Brumley wants his paperwork complete, assembled, and wearing the new cover.
  9. Return what you borrow. It's good manners, and good karma is worth points.
  10. Finished work goes to the corner office. That's also where the paychecks live.
  11. Be friendly, even when it hurts. Say hello to the front desk on your way out. Yes, also to the consultants.
  12. Productivity takes many forms. Your desk computer came with more than spreadsheets. A man who has never won at cards on company time has never truly worked.
At Home 17 pts
  1. One man's trash is another man's treasure. The dumpster behind Shady Pines has been feeding raccoons for years. Read the fine print on anything you find in there. Some deals expire today.
  2. Know your enemy. You can't fix a car you haven't looked inside.
  3. That blinking light isn't a disco ball. Somebody called. Several somebodies. One of them knows something about your wallet that you don't.
  4. A couch is a piggy bank with cushions. Every quarter counts this weekend.
  5. Know thyself. Before a big night, take a long, honest look at what you're working with.
  6. A gentleman's medicine cabinet holds the two secret weapons of 1970s romance. Apply both as directed. On the outside of the body.
— 9 —
At The Warehouse 40 pts
  1. Lift with your wheels, not your back. Car parts are heavy, and nobody sells you one you can't carry away. Grab your transportation before you go inside.
  2. Membership has its privileges, and its expiration dates. Your answering machine may have mentioned something. Bring money.
  3. Always say hello to the greeter. He has been standing there since the Carter administration. He's earned a chat.
  4. Bond with your fellow man. Every warehouse has a wall of big-screen TVs and a row of strangers in front of it, arms crossed, nodding. Be one of them.
  5. Free samples are free. Chat with the nice lady in the hairnet, then actually try the product.
  6. Sample ladies give away more than food. A man with a stain shaped like a Midwestern state should mention it to someone who sells the cure, and then use it.
  7. Batteries live where the tires live. The tire guy takes coupons, even greasy ones. Hand yours over and save.
  8. The $1.50 hot dog is sacred. Buy one, and eat it with the care of a man wearing his only good suit.
  9. Mind the clock. The warehouse closes at 8:30 PM sharp. Plan your errands accordingly.
The Victory Lap 30 pts
  1. Civilization depends on returned carts. Leave the warehouse with a cart in hand and Jeff will put it back like a decent human being.
  2. Batteries don't install themselves. Red to red, black to black.
  3. Take her out for a spin. A man who just fixed his own car deserves a victory lap, and gas is cheap.
  4. Sleep is earned. Paid, fixed and spotless? The waterbed awaits. Not one minute before.
Money Talks

Jeff starts the weekend with $12.75. Between a paycheck and whatever the furniture coughs up, a careful man can afford everything on his list with change to spare. A careless man rides the bus to his own reunion.

Hazards Of Modern Living

Like all fine adventure games, Leisure Suit Jeff has many exciting ways for Jeff to die. Polyester Software accepts no responsibility for any of them. A few friendly reminders:

  • The office fridge holds other people's food. Some of those people keep count.
  • Never drink coffee that is older than your car.
  • Cologne goes on the outside. Even the economy-size bottle. Especially the economy-size bottle.
  • Condiments are not a meal, no matter how big the jar.
  • Stores close. A man who hasn't bought his car battery by closing time is a man taking the bus to his reunion.
Save Early, Save Often

Type SAVE to save your game, or SAVE and a name, like SAVE BEFORE LUNCH. Type RESTORE with the same name to go back. Your saves are kept on this computer.

Did Jeff just die? Type UNDO to take back your last move. If only real life worked this way. RESTART starts the whole weekend over.

Music, Score & Saving

Music & Sound

Every location has its own theme, played on the AdLib FM sound chip, with jingles for points and a dirge for when Jeff dies. If people in the next cubicle are listening:

SOUND OFF / SOUND ON turns everything off or on.
MUSIC OFF / SFX OFF turn off only the music or only the sound effects.
VOLUME 5 sets the volume, from 0 to 10.
Or click the ♪ button next to the prompt.

Keeping Score

Type SCORE to see your points and how many moves you've made. Points come from doing the right thing, the nice thing, and every so often the dumb-but-satisfying thing.

Keeping Time

Type TIME for the time. The weekend starts at 2:30 PM on Friday. Act I ends when Jeff goes to bed with his chores done.

Stuck?

Reread pages 9 and 10. LOOK at everything again. Talk to everyone again. Still stuck? Type HINT. Jeff will tell you what he'd do next.
